---
title: Origins
description: What the sources say about how old deq is, and the earliest drawings of Kurdish tattoos.
---

No one knows where deq began. Wikipedia traces it to ancient Mesopotamia <Cite ids="wikipedia-deq" />, and Kurdistan Chronicle says it predates Christianity and Islam <Cite ids="kurdistan-chronicle-2026" />.

Diplomatic Magazine says experts date the practice to about 10,000 years ago, and that it was strongest among nomadic Kurds and Ezidis <Cite ids="diplomatic-xal" />. The article gives no source for that figure, and the other sources say only that deq is ancient or older than Christianity <Cite ids="wikipedia-deq kurdistan-chronicle-2026" />.

## Ink and marks in old texts

Aëtius of Amida, born in Amed (Diyarbakır), recorded a tattoo ink of pine bark, corroded bronze and leek juice in the early 6th century <Cite ids="wikipedia-deq" />. Özgür Politika names his work _Medicae Artis Principes_ and gives the full recipe <Cite ids="ozgur-2021" />.

Article 226 of Hammurabi's code is linked to the marking of slaves <Cite ids="wikipedia-deq ozgur-2021" />. Özgür Politika also links Babylonian slave marks with the star of Ishtar in the Eanna temple at Uruk <Cite ids="ozgur-2021" />.

## Early drawings

Engravings attributed to Jacques de Morgan's _Mission scientifique en Perse_ show the tattoos of Kurdish women from Tchômaklou, Kilakân and Kialhours <Cite ids="de-morgan-mission" />. The attribution comes from the images themselves and has not yet been checked against the publication <Cite ids="de-morgan-mission" />.
