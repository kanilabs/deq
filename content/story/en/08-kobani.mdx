In 2015 National Geographic published "The Last Tattooed Women of Kobane", with photographs by Jodi Hilton <Cite ids="natgeo-2015" />. Hilton also published the essay on her own site <Cite ids="hilton-tattooed-women" />.

The essay says the practice reached Syria from Urfa through Kobanî, and that girls were tattooed between about 8 and 12, often by travelling women <Cite ids="natgeo-2015" />. In it, an inverted V on the chin marks the family group, and a moon near the eye can mark a conversion from Ezidism to Islam <Cite ids="natgeo-2015" />.

<Figure id="kobani-woman-portrait-01" />

Both essays say the practice had largely stopped about fifty years earlier <Cite ids="natgeo-2015 hilton-tattooed-women" />.

Surprising Lives retold the story in 2015, drawing on these essays <Cite ids="surprisinglives-2015" />. The Getty Images event page and a 2014 article in The National could not be read for this project <Cite ids="getty-kobani pressreader-national-2014" />.
