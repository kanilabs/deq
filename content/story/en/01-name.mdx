In Kurmanji the tattoo is called _deq_, and it is also known as _xał_ <Cite ids="wikipedia-deq" />. SBS uses both words, writing "deq/xal" <Cite ids="sbs-2022" />. In Sorani, _xał_ also means a dot or a full stop, and a maternal uncle <Cite ids="diplomatic-xal" />.

## Where the word comes from

Wikipedia traces _deq_ to Syriac _Daqqe_ or _Dyoqo_, "puncturing" <Cite ids="wikipedia-deq" />, while Kurdistan Chronicle gives the source as Aramaic _Dyoqo_ <Cite ids="kurdistan-chronicle-2026" />. The two sources disagree on the language.

## People and other names

A tattooer is a _deqqak_ (a man) or _deqqake_ (a woman), and a tattooed person is _medquk_ or _medquke_ <Cite ids="wikipedia-deq" />. The Amargi calls practitioners "deqqars" <Cite ids="amargi-2025" />, and the artist Elu signs as "DEKKAKE" <Cite ids="instagram-deq-ttt" />.

In Arabic the practice is called _vesm_ or _veshm_ <Cite ids="wikipedia-deq" />, also written _wesm_ <Cite ids="indyturk-2020" />. Around Şanlıurfa, Turkish-language terms include _dek_, _daki_, _vesm_ and _döğün_, according to the deq-ttt archive's listing of Ahmet Bikiç's 2017 documentary, which this project has not yet seen <Cite ids="deq-ttt-archive" />.
